# Targeted research: poor TRAIN fit, loss gradients and small-batch inference

Consulted7 October2026 after completed E5/E6 rejection and actual learned-loss diagnostic. Existing E7/E8 remains frozen. These sources supply hypotheses, not transferred performance claims.

Title: Rethinking "Batch" in BatchNorm. Authors: Yuxin Wu and Justin Johnson. Year:2021. Source: [author preprint, full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07576).

Problem: normalization batch construction and inaccurate population statistics can affect inference. Method: analyze EMA statistics, recompute fixed-model population statistics (PreciseBN), and study batch/domain inconsistencies. Dataset: ImageNet classification among other visual-recognition tasks. Important result: Table2's normalization-batch-size2 ResNet50 has validation error35.5% with EMA versus32.0% using the reported precise-statistics configurations. Relevant insight: training batch size and inference statistics need separate examination. Application here: the existing fracture U-Net uses BatchNorm with training batch2; measure stored-stat versus current-input-stat behavior on TRAIN before a controlled TRAIN-only statistics candidate. Current action: diagnostic only; no BatchNorm candidate or validation statistics fitting. Limitations: nonmedical tasks and different network/training scale; no promised fracture gain. Small-batch recalibration itself can accumulate errors. Reserved-test and validation data must never estimate statistics.

Title: Generalised Dice overlap as a deep learning loss function for highly unbalanced segmentations. Authors: Carole H. Sudre, Wenqi Li, Tom Vercauteren, Sebastien Ourselin and M. Jorge Cardoso. Year:2017. Source: [author manuscript, full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03237).

Problem: rare foreground and sensitivity of segmentation losses to imbalance and learning rate. Method: compare weighted cross-entropy, Dice, sensitivity/specificity loss and inverse-volume Generalized Dice across2D/3D networks and sampling contexts. Dataset: BRATS tumor segmentation and age-related white-matter hyperintensities. Important result: reports greater Generalized Dice robustness across its imbalance/configuration comparisons. Relevant insight: evaluate gradient behavior and empty-label handling instead of assuming all Dice formulations are equivalent. Application here: existing TRAIN foreground is extremely sparse; sampled learned normal Dice gradients were nearly absent while BCE remained active. Current action: derivative and learned-gradient diagnostics, not a new loss implementation. Limitations: brain MRI rather than radiographs; training patches required foreground, unlike MedVision's many normal full images. Inverse-volume weighting needs explicit handling of empty foreground. No published result is a local model metric.
