# Targeted primary-source research: sparse fracture masks and poor TRAIN fit

Consulted 8 October 2026 while the registered E9/E10 hard-example pair was running. This record changes no model, experiment protocol, training code, sampling, data, or checkpoint. Any intervention below is a hypothesis for consideration only after the completed hard pair and its native failure analysis. Published results are not local MedVision results or promises of 90% performance. No external dataset or weights were downloaded.

The project evidence inspected for this research is the current `HARD_EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.json`, `AI_TARGET_REPRESENTATION_20261007/summary.json`, `AI_LEARNED_LOSS_GRADIENTS_E5_v3_unaugmented/completion.json`, `AI_SHARED_LOSS_GRADIENTS_E8_v3_r2/completion.json`, and E7's raw TRAIN fit metrics. TRAIN has 493 fracture images and 2,315 normal images. At 256 pixels the natural-image foreground fraction is 0.00086179 (0.08618%); no positive image or annotation instance vanished in the representation audit. The corrected 16-case E5 diagnostic measured a normal Dice logit-gradient median of 3.54e-10 versus BCE 1.20e-4. These sampled eval derivatives are not a whole-TRAIN population estimate or a causal explanation. E7 and E8's poor raw TRAIN fits justify investigating learning, rather than assuming only validation domain shift. The presence branch's large sampled shared gradients also do not prove harmful interference by themselves.

1. **FracAtlas: A Dataset for Fracture Classification, Localization and Segmentation of Musculoskeletal Radiographs.** Authors: Iftekharul Abedeen, Md. Ashiqur Rahman, Fatema Zohra Prottyasha, Tasnim Ahmed, Tareque Mohmud Chowdhury and Swakkhar Shatabda. Year: 2023. Source: [Scientific Data, DOI10.1038/s41597-023-02432-4, publisher PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02432-4.pdf).

   Problem/method/dataset: document 4,083 radiographs and validate COCO-pretrained YOLOv8s detection and YOLOv8s-seg segmentation. Technical validation used only fractured images: 574 TRAIN, 82 VAL, 61 TEST, 600-pixel input, 30 epochs. Important result: published mask precision was 83%, recall 49.9%, mAP50 58.9%. Relevance/application: fracture-mask recall was limited even with pretrained higher-resolution segmentation; these results provide context, not a MedVision comparator. Source describes fracture-region polygons and retained logos/text; neither validates the cause of our normal-edge false alarms. Limitations: fracture-only evaluation supplies no specificity on normal images; different split, architecture, resolution and metrics. Do not substitute mAP for case accuracy or native positive Dice. Access: readable 8-page publisher PDF; inspected annotation/technical-validation text and Table 2 information. Publisher HTML initially errored and PMC required a browser check.

2. **nnU-Net: a self-configuring method for deep learning-based biomedical image segmentation.** Authors: Fabian Isensee, Paul F. Jaeger, Simon A. A. Kohl, Jens Petersen and Klaus H. Maier-Hein. Year: 2021 journal issue; online 7 December 2020. Sources: [Nature Methods article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-020-01008-z), official [trainer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/master/nnunetv2/training/nnUNetTrainer/nnUNetTrainer.py), [data loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/master/nnunetv2/training/dataloading/data_loader.py) and [Dice implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MIC-DKFZ/nnUNet/master/nnunetv2/training/loss/dice.py).

   Problem/method/dataset/result: self-configures medical segmentation pipelines; article abstract reports exceeding most specialized approaches across 23 public challenge datasets. Relevant implementation: inspected current official code sets foreground oversampling to 0.33, preserves random patches, and falls back when a selected case has no foreground. At batch size 2 its rounding makes one slot foreground-directed. CE+Dice is retained; `batch_dice` explicitly controls summation across images. Application: foreground exposure and overlap reduction are separate factors; neither requires excluding all normal images. Limitations: directed *patch* sampling is not guaranteed positive-*case* sampling in our full-image loader; current default code is not a frozen reproduction of the 2021 study. No fracture-specific gain follows. Access: article abstract/metadata only; attempted PDF redirected to subscription preview. Full official source files were readable, with relevant sections inspected.

3. **On the dice loss gradient and the ways to mimic it.** Authors: Hoel Kervadec and Marleen de Bruijne. Year:2023. Source: [author arXiv manuscript2304.04319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04319), also readable [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.04319v1).

   Problem/method/dataset: analytically examine soft-Dice gradients and compare signed gradient-map supervision using ENet/residual U-Net on ACDC cardiac MRI and Promise12 prostate MRI. Important result: their losses produced broadly similar segmentation and convergence; this was an explanation study, not proof a new loss is superior. Relevant insight: gradient sign, reduction and combination matter; the binary task still requires pressure against predictions outside the object. Application: interpret near-constant normal-image Dice alongside BCE and test a clearly defined shape-loss weighting/reduction if the current pair still underfits. Limitations: preprint, MRI targets and different loss/optimizer setup; large or small sampled gradient norms alone do not predict Adam trajectories. Access:11-page PDF and complete HTML were readable; inspected definitions, derivatives, experimental setup, results/table and conclusion.

4. **On the dice loss variants and sub-patching.** Authors: Hoel Kervadec and Marleen de Bruijne. Year:2023. Sources: [official MIDL2023 short-paper page](https://2023.midl.io/papers/s091), [OpenReview manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=d1O5xjKX_yd).

   Problem/method: analyzes Dice variants when crops contain no foreground. Important result/insight: the accessible abstract reports difficulties handling empty-patch denominators and more suitable behavior of their mime formulation. Application: before any crop or alternate Dice candidate, define empty-target behavior and test finite losses and gradients. Limitations: short paper; detailed datasets, comparisons and numerical results were not independently reviewed and are deliberately not reported. Access: official conference page and author publication abstract readable; indexed manuscript first-page extract visible, but direct OpenReview PDF showed browser verification. No claim of full-paper access.

5. **Optimization for Medical Image Segmentation: Theory and Practice when evaluating with Dice Score or Jaccard Index.** Authors: Tom Eelbode, Jeroen Bertels, Maxim Berman, Dirk Vandermeulen, Frederik Maes, Raf Bisschops and Matthew B. Blaschko. Year:2020. Source: [author manuscript2010.13499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.13499), IEEE TMI39(11),3679–3690.

   Problem/method/dataset: theoretical loss/metric relationships plus comparisons on BRATS, ISLES2017/2018, dental molars, colorectal polyps and WMH. Important result: metric-sensitive losses generally beat CE-based alternatives for Dice/IoU in their experiments; fixed CE weighting did not consistently surrogate overlap across object sizes. Application: retain an overlap objective when testing learning improvements; hard-example weighting should not be assumed to solve sparse-pixel learning or localization. Limitations: MedVision already uses BCE+Dice, so this supports inspecting reduction/relative contribution, not claiming the loss is absent. Their training included CE pretraining and different architectures, modalities and schedules. Access:15-page PDF readable; inspected theory conclusions, datasets, training, lesion-size findings and final results/conclusion. No local gain is inferred.

6. **Convolutional Neural Networks for Medical Image Analysis: Full Training or Fine Tuning?** Authors: Nima Tajbakhsh, Jae Y. Shin, Suryakanth R. Gurudu, R. Todd Hurst, Christopher B.    Problem/method/dataset: compare scratch AlexNet with layer-wise ImageNet fine-tuning for polyp detection, CT pulmonary embolism detection, colonoscopy quality classification and ultrasound intima-media boundary segmentation. Important result: adequately tuned pretrained models equaled or exceeded scratch models across their applications; shallow fine-tuning could underperform. Limitations: AlexNet patch classification/interface tracing is not AttentionUNet fracture-mask segmentation; architecture changes would confound a pretrained-versus-scratch claim. Current AttentionUNet has no directly compatible ImageNet encoder weights. Access:17-page manuscript readable; inspected segmentation design/results and fine-tuning/convergence discussion; PubMed confirms publication year.

## Hypotheses to consider after E9/E10 analysis

The lowest-disruption loss hypothesis is to strengthen useful positive-image overlap supervision while keeping BCE on every normal and fractured image. This is an inference from the project's verified reduction/derivative evidence, informed by entries 2, 3 and 5; none proves positive-only Dice weighting is best for fractures. A separately registered candidate could change one fixed positive-Dice coefficient, preserving sampling, architecture, all-image BCE, input size and native inference. Factor 1 must reproduce the reference scalar and gradients; tests must cover zero-positive batches, empty masks, tiny masks and invalid augmentation pixels. Normal examples must not be removed or made gradient-free. Compare native precision, recall, Dice, IoU, useful coverage and false-positive pixels; reject mere positive overlap gains accompanied by unreliable case alerts.

An alternative *separate* hypothesis is batch Dice versus image Dice, motivated by entry 2's explicit reduction. Mixed batches make normal predictions contribute to a shared foreground denominator; all-normal batches still require a defined finite objective. This is not equivalent to positive-Dice weighting and must not be combined with it in one supposedly single-factor experiment. At the natural positive-image prevalence 493/2808, most batch-size-2 draws contain no positive image, so batch reduction alone is not guaranteed to fix foreground exposure.

Foreground-directed crops or positive-case scheduling would change the training distribution and image context. Entry2 supplies an implementation precedent, not fracture-specific evidence or permission to duplicate hard images repeatedly. Any such experiment must preserve representative normals, log unique exposures and evaluate full unaltered validation images. A crop policy requiring a ground-truth region at inference would be invalid for this automatic task.

If loss/exposure experiments still cannot fit TRAIN, transfer initialization becomes a targeted alternative. A new encoder must first be compared with its own scratch control, so architecture and initialization are distinguishable. Same-architecture TRAIN-only reconstruction pretraining avoids that architecture confound, but needs real measured evidence and may cost substantial compute. No pretrained checkpoint availability, licensing, compatibility, or improvement is assumed here.

The frozen 463-image validation set remains the development comparator. Calibration and reserved-test predictions must stay unavailable until the existing gates pass. This record does not authorize threshold grids, classifier gating of fracture masks, an external data download, or production promotion. E9/E10 continue under their existing protocol.
